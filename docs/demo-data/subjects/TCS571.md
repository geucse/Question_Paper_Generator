## Course outcomes

- **CO1** — Create and adapt visualizations to represent complex data sets and emphasize targeted concepts for effective communication
- **CO2** — Analyze and interpret large volumes of data to identify patterns, trends, and insights.
- **CO3** — Apply data visualization techniques to communicate complex data sets effectively.
- **CO4** — Develop skills in storytelling with data, effectively conveying narratives through visual representations.
- **CO5** — Demonstrate proficiency in using tools and technologies for big data visualization.
- **CO6** — Use leading open-source and commercial software packages (Tableau) to create and publish visualizations that enable clear interpretations of big, complex, and real-world data

### Q1

**MCQ · 1 mark · easy · Remember · CO1 · Unit 1: “The eight hats of data visualization design”**

In the eight hats of data visualization design, which role focuses on how visual choices affect human perception, attention and memory?

- (A) The initiator
- (B) The data scientist
- (C) The cognitive scientist
- (D) The project manager

**Answer:** (C) The cognitive scientist

**Model answer:** The cognitive scientist brings knowledge of perception and cognition to design decisions.

### Q2

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Understand · CO1, CO3 · Unit 1: “The visualization's function-explain, explore, exhibit”**

Distinguish visualizations whose function is to explain, to explore and to exhibit, giving one example of each from university admissions data.


**Model answer:** Explain: the designer leads the reader to a finding (annotated chart showing that applications doubled after a new programme). Explore: the user investigates freely (interactive filters by state and programme). Exhibit: data shown for reference without a narrative (a table of seats per course).

### Q3

**True/False · 1 mark · easy · Understand · CO1 · Unit 1: “Metaphor Visualization”**

True or False: Metaphor visualization organises abstract information using a familiar visual structure, such as a map, a tree or a road, to aid understanding.


**Answer:** True

**Model answer:** True; the metaphor transfers the reader's understanding of a familiar structure to the information.

### Q4

**Short Answer · 3 marks · easy · Understand · CO1 · Unit 1: “key factors in a visualization project”**

List four key factors that should be settled at the start of a visualization project and explain how one of them constrains design choices.


**Model answer:** Purpose/intent, audience, data availability and quality, format and medium, time and resources. Example: a printed annual report cannot use interactivity, so annotation and arrangement must carry the message.

### Q5

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Analyze · CO2 · Unit 2: “physical properties of data”**

Explain how the physical properties of data (categorical, ordinal or quantitative values; range and distribution) influence the choice of visual encoding, with one example each.


**Model answer:** Categorical: distinct hues or positions, no implied order (product types). Ordinal: ordered positions or sequential colour (grades A-E). Quantitative: position or length on a scale (sales). A wide or skewed range may need a log scale or binning (city populations).

### Q6

**MCQ · 2 marks · medium · Understand · CO3 · Unit 2: “degree of accuracy in interpretation”**

Which visual encoding allows readers to compare quantitative values most accurately?

- (A) Area of circles
- (B) Colour saturation
- (C) Position along a common scale
- (D) Angle of pie slices

**Answer:** (C) Position along a common scale

**Model answer:** Perception studies rank position on a common aligned scale as the most accurately judged encoding.

### Q7

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Apply · CO4 · Unit 2: “Interactivity, Annotation, and Arrangement”**

A monthly fee-collection line chart is to be shown to the finance committee. Suggest three annotations and one arrangement decision that would make its message clear.


**Model answer:** Annotate the target line, label the lowest month with its cause (e.g. a portal outage), and add a short headline stating the trend. Arrangement: place the chart first with supporting breakdowns below, aligned to the same time axis.

### Q8

**Case Study · 10 marks · hard · Analyze · CO2, CO4 · Unit 2: “Using visual analysis to find stories”**

> **Case:** A city has hourly air-quality readings (PM2.5, PM10, NO2) from 25 monitoring stations for three years, with station locations, traffic counts and dates of festivals and policy changes.

Describe how you would use visual analysis to find stories in the case data, which views you would build first, and how you would turn one finding into a narrative for the public.


**Model answer:** Start with distributions and time series per pollutant, small multiples by station, a map of station averages, and scatter plots of PM2.5 against traffic. Look for seasonality, outliers (festival spikes), before/after policy changes. Build a narrative: question, evidence (annotated time series and map), context, conclusion, with clear caveats on data gaps.

### Q9

**Assertion-Reason · 2 marks · medium · Analyze · CO3 · Unit 3: “Choropleth map”**

Read the assertion and reason about the choropleth map and choose the correct option.

- **Assertion (A):** A choropleth map should usually show rates or densities rather than raw totals.
- **Reason (R):** Larger or more populous regions accumulate larger totals, which distorts comparisons between regions.
- (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- (B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
- (C) A is true, but R is false
- (D) A is false, but R is true

**Answer:** (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

**Model answer:** Both true and R explains A: normalising (per capita, per km2) makes regions comparable.

### Q10

**MCQ · 1 mark · easy · Apply · CO3 · Unit 3: “Showing changes over time”**

Which chart is designed to show a stock's daily open, high, low and close prices over time?

- (A) Candlestick chart
- (B) Treemap
- (C) Sankey diagram
- (D) Square pie

**Answer:** (A) Candlestick chart

**Model answer:** A candlestick chart encodes open, high, low and close for each period.

### Q11

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Evaluate · CO3 · Unit 3: “Assessing hierarchies and part-to-whole relationships”**

A department's budget is split into 6 schools and 30 sub-heads. Evaluate a pie chart and a treemap for showing this hierarchy and recommend one.


**Model answer:** A pie chart handles only a few slices and one level; 30 sub-heads would be unreadable and angles are judged poorly. A treemap shows both levels, scales to many items and uses area within nested rectangles. Recommend the treemap, with labels for the largest items.

### Q12

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Apply · CO2 · Unit 3: “Plotting connections and relationships”**

Explain what a scatter plot matrix shows and how you would use it to examine the relationships among attendance, internal marks, assignment scores and end-semester marks.


**Model answer:** A grid of scatter plots of every pair of variables (with distributions on the diagonal). Scan it for correlations (e.g. internal vs end-semester marks), clusters and outliers, then examine strong pairs in detail.

### Q13

**Short Answer · 6 marks · hard · Evaluate · CO5 · Unit 4: “Tools for data visualization”**

Evaluate Tableau, Google Charts and Datawrapper for two needs: (a) a newsroom publishing quick charts on its website, and (b) an enterprise sales team needing interactive dashboards over several data sources. Recommend a tool for each.


**Model answer:** (a) Datawrapper: browser-based, fast, publication-ready charts and maps without code; Google Charts suits teams with web developers. (b) Tableau: connects to many sources, blending, interactive dashboards, sharing. Recommend Datawrapper for (a) and Tableau for (b).

### Q16

**Short Answer · 3 marks · easy · Understand · CO5 · Unit 4: “Tools for data visualization”**

List four criteria you would use to select a data visualization tool for a university department.


**Model answer:** Supported data sources and data size; interactivity and sharing options; ease of use and training needed; cost and licensing; security and data-privacy controls.

### Q17

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Analyze · CO6 · Unit 5: “Data Blending”**

Compare a join and data blending in Tableau: when is each performed, at what level of detail, and when would you choose blending?


**Model answer:** A join combines tables row by row in the data source before aggregation. Blending queries each source separately, aggregates at the view's level of detail, then combines on linking fields (like a left join of aggregates). Choose blending for different databases or different granularities, e.g. monthly targets against daily sales.

### Q18

**Numerical · 1 mark · easy · Apply · CO6 · Unit 5: “Table calculations”**

Quarterly sales are Q1 = 20, Q2 = 30, Q3 = 25 and Q4 = 25. What value does a 'percent of total' table calculation show for Q2?


**Answer:** 30 %

**Model answer:** 30 / (20 + 30 + 25 + 25) = 30 / 100 = 30%.

### Q19

**MCQ · 2 marks · medium · Understand · CO6 · Unit 5: “Granularity and Level of detail”**

In Tableau, what does a FIXED level-of-detail expression such as {FIXED [Region] : SUM([Sales])} compute?

- (A) Sales at whatever dimensions are in the view
- (B) Sales per region, regardless of the dimensions in the view
- (C) A running total across all regions
- (D) The average of sales across regions

**Answer:** (B) Sales per region, regardless of the dimensions in the view

**Model answer:** FIXED computes the aggregate at the dimensions listed, independent of the view.

### Q20

**Long Answer · 8 marks · hard · Create · CO4, CO6 · Unit 5: “Create Interactive Dashboards”**

Design an interactive Tableau dashboard for admissions staff to monitor applications by programme, state and week. Specify the views, filters, dashboard actions and one dual-axis chart, and explain how the design supports the staff's decisions.


**Model answer:** Views: KPI cards (applications, offers, conversion), weekly applications line with offers on a dual axis, map by state, bar chart by programme. Filters: session, programme type. Actions: clicking a state filters the other views; hovering highlights a programme. The dual axis compares applications and offer rate over time, showing where follow-up is needed.
