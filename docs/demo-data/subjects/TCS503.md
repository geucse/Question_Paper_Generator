### Q1

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Understand · CO1, CO2 · Unit 1: “Three-schema architecture and data independence”**

Explain the three-schema architecture and distinguish logical data independence from physical data independence with one example of each.


**Model answer:** External (user views), conceptual (whole logical schema) and internal (storage) levels with mappings between them. Physical independence: adding an index or changing file organisation does not change the conceptual schema. Logical independence: adding a column to a table does not change existing external views.

### Q2

**MCQ · 1 mark · easy · Remember · CO1 · Unit 1: “Advantages of using DBMS approach”**

Which of the following is NOT an advantage of the DBMS approach over a file system?

- (A) Controlled redundancy
- (B) Concurrent access with consistency
- (C) Lower cost and overhead for every small application
- (D) Backup and recovery

**Answer:** (C) Lower cost and overhead for every small application

**Model answer:** A DBMS adds cost and overhead; for very small, simple applications a file system can be cheaper.

### Q3

**True/False · 1 mark · easy · Understand · CO1 · Unit 1: “Data models, schemas, and instances”**

True or False: A database schema changes frequently, while the database instance rarely changes.


**Answer:** False

**Model answer:** False. The schema (intension) is stable; the instance (extension, the data at a moment) changes with every update.

### Q5

**Short Answer · 3 marks · easy · Understand · CO2 · Unit 2: “Weak Entity Types”**

What is a weak entity type? Explain its identifying relationship and partial key using DEPENDENT of EMPLOYEE as an example.


**Model answer:** A weak entity has no key of its own; it is identified through an owner entity via an identifying relationship (total participation). DEPENDENT is identified by the owner EMPLOYEE's SSN plus its partial key Dependent_name.

### Q6

**MCQ · 2 marks · medium · Apply · CO2 · Unit 2: “Roles and Structural Constraints”**

Every employee works in exactly one department and a department can have many employees. Which description of WORKS_FOR is correct?

- (A) 1:1, partial participation of EMPLOYEE
- (B) N:1 (EMPLOYEE:DEPARTMENT), total participation of EMPLOYEE
- (C) M:N, total participation of both
- (D) 1:N (EMPLOYEE:DEPARTMENT), partial participation of EMPLOYEE

**Answer:** (B) N:1 (EMPLOYEE:DEPARTMENT), total participation of EMPLOYEE

**Model answer:** Many employees to one department (N:1); every employee must participate (total).

### Q7

**Case Study · 10 marks · hard · Create · CO5, CO6 · Unit 2: “Entity Types, Entity Sets, Attributes and Keys”**

> **Case:** A hospital records patients (ID, name, date of birth, phone numbers), doctors (ID, name, specialisation) and wards (number, capacity). A patient is admitted to one ward at a time, may be treated by several doctors, and each doctor treats many patients; each treatment has a date and a diagnosis.

Design an ER diagram for the case, identifying entity types, attributes, keys, relationships, cardinality ratios and participation constraints. State any assumptions.


**Model answer:** Entities PATIENT (PatientID key, Name, DOB, multivalued Phone), DOCTOR (DoctorID key, Name, Specialisation), WARD (WardNo key, Capacity). ADMITTED_TO PATIENT N:1 WARD (partial for PATIENT if out-patients exist). TREATS DOCTOR M:N PATIENT with relationship attributes Date and Diagnosis.

### Q8

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Analyze · CO2 · Unit 2: “Relationship types of degree higher than two”**

A ternary relationship SUPPLY(Supplier, Part, Project) is replaced by three binary relationships SUPPLIES(Supplier, Part), USES(Project, Part) and CAN_SUPPLY(Supplier, Project). Explain, with an example, why information can be lost.


**Model answer:** The binaries record pairs only. If s1 supplies p1 to j2 and p2 to j1, the binaries also suggest s1 supplies p1 to j1, which may be false: the ternary fact (s, p, j) cannot be reconstructed from the three pairs.

### Q9

**Programming · 6 marks · medium · Apply · CO3 · Unit 3: “More complex SQL Queries”**

Given EMPLOYEE(eid, name, dept_id, salary) and DEPARTMENT(dept_id, dname), write SQL to list (a) the names of departments whose average salary exceeds 50000 and (b) the employees who earn more than the average salary of their own department.

*Language:* SQL

**Model answer:**

```sql
(a) SELECT d.dname FROM DEPARTMENT d JOIN EMPLOYEE e ON e.dept_id = d.dept_id GROUP BY d.dname HAVING AVG(e.salary) > 50000;
(b) SELECT e.name FROM EMPLOYEE e WHERE e.salary > (SELECT AVG(x.salary) FROM EMPLOYEE x WHERE x.dept_id = e.dept_id);
```

### Q10

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Apply · CO3 · Unit 3: “Binary Relational Operations: JOIN and DIVISION”**

Using TAKES(sid, cid) and COURSE(cid, title), write a relational algebra expression for the IDs of students who have taken every course, and explain the division operator used.


**Model answer:** TAKES / pi_cid(COURSE). Division returns the sid values that are paired in TAKES with every cid in pi_cid(COURSE).

### Q11

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Apply · CO2, CO5 · Unit 3: “ER- to-Relational Mapping”**

Map to relations: a weak entity DEPENDENT (partial key name) owned by EMPLOYEE (key ssn), and an M:N relationship WORKS_ON between EMPLOYEE and PROJECT (key pno) with attribute hours. Give primary and foreign keys.


**Model answer:** DEPENDENT(ssn, name, ...), PK (ssn, name), FK ssn -> EMPLOYEE. WORKS_ON(ssn, pno, hours), PK (ssn, pno), FKs ssn -> EMPLOYEE and pno -> PROJECT.

### Q12

**Programming · 6 marks · hard · Apply · CO3, CO4 · Unit 3: “Specifying constraints as Assertion and Trigger”**

Write an SQL trigger on EMPLOYEE(eid, name, salary) that rejects any update reducing an employee's salary, and explain why this rule is better enforced in the database than in each application.

*Language:* SQL

**Model answer:**

```sql
CREATE TRIGGER no_salary_cut BEFORE UPDATE OF salary ON EMPLOYEE FOR EACH ROW WHEN (NEW.salary < OLD.salary) BEGIN SIGNAL SQLSTATE '45000' SET MESSAGE_TEXT = 'Salary cannot be reduced'; END; (syntax varies by DBMS). The database enforces it for every application and ad hoc update, so the integrity rule cannot be bypassed.
```

### Q13

**Numerical · 4 marks · hard · Analyze · CO2, CO5 · Unit 4: “Functional Dependencies”**

R(A, B, C, D, E) has functional dependencies A -> B, B -> C, CD -> E, E -> A. How many candidate keys does R have?


**Answer:** 4

**Model answer:** D is on no right-hand side, so every key contains D; D alone gives only D. AD, BD, CD and ED each determine all attributes, and no proper subset does: 4 candidate keys.

### Q14

**Short Answer · 5 marks · hard · Analyze · CO5 · Unit 4: “Boyce-Codd Normal Form”**

R(Student, Course, Instructor) has FDs {Student, Course} -> Instructor and Instructor -> Course. Show that R is in 3NF but not in BCNF, and give a BCNF decomposition. What is lost?


**Model answer:** Keys: {Student, Course} and {Student, Instructor}. Instructor -> Course has a non-superkey determinant, violating BCNF, but Course is prime, so 3NF holds. Decompose into (Instructor, Course) and (Student, Instructor): lossless, but {Student, Course} -> Instructor is no longer preserved.

### Q15

**MCQ · 1 mark · easy · Remember · CO2 · Unit 4: “General Definitions of Second and Third Normal Forms”**

A relation in which a non-prime attribute depends on only part of a composite candidate key violates which normal form?

- (A) First normal form
- (B) Second normal form
- (C) Boyce-Codd normal form only
- (D) Fourth normal form

**Answer:** (B) Second normal form

**Model answer:** A partial dependency of a non-prime attribute violates 2NF.

### Q16

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Apply · CO5 · Unit 4: “Properties of Relational Decompositions”**

R(A, B, C) with F = {A -> B} is decomposed into R1(A, B) and R2(A, C). Is the decomposition lossless? Is it dependency preserving? Justify both.


**Model answer:** Lossless: R1 intersect R2 = {A}, and A -> B, so A is a key of R1. Dependency preserving: A -> B lies within R1.

### Q17

**Fill in the Blank · 2 marks · easy · Remember · CO4 · Unit 5: “The ACID Properties”**

The ___ property ensures that either all operations of a transaction are reflected in the database or none are, while ___ ensures that the effects of a committed transaction survive system crashes.


**Answer:** blank 1: atomicity; blank 2: durability

**Model answer:** Atomicity and durability.

### Q18

**Short Answer · 5 marks · medium · Analyze · CO4 · Unit 5: “2PL, Serializability and Recoverability”**

Is the schedule r1(X), w2(X), w1(X) conflict serializable? Draw the precedence graph and justify your answer.


**Model answer:** r1(X) before w2(X) gives T1 -> T2; w2(X) before w1(X) gives T2 -> T1. The precedence graph has a cycle, so the schedule is not conflict serializable.

### Q20

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Understand · CO4 · Unit 5: “Checkpointing”**

Explain how checkpoints in the log reduce the work of recovering from a system crash.


**Model answer:** At a checkpoint the DBMS forces log records and dirty pages to disk and writes a checkpoint record listing active transactions. After a crash, recovery starts from the last checkpoint: committed transactions before it need no redo, and only the log after it (plus active transactions) is scanned.
