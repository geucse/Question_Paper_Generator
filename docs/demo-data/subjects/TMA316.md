## Course outcomes

- **CO1** — Be able to specify and manipulate basic mathematical objects such as sets, functions, and relations . Demonstrate an understanding of partial order relations and Lattices.
- **CO2** — Understand the basics of discrete probability and number theory, and be able to apply the methods from these subjects in problem solving.
- **CO3** — Produce convincing arguments, conceive and/or analyze basic mathematical proofs and discriminate between valid and unreliable arguments.
- **CO4** — Discriminate, identify and prove the properties of groups and subgroups
- **CO5** — Be able to apply basic counting techniques to solve combinatorial problems
- **CO6** — Demonstrate different traversal methods for trees and graphs. Model problems in Computer Science using graphs and trees.

### Q1

**Proof · 6 marks · medium · Apply · CO1, CO3 · Unit 1: “equivalence relation”**

Let R be the relation on the integers defined by a R b if and only if 5 divides (a - b). Prove that R is an equivalence relation and describe its equivalence classes.


**Model answer:** Reflexive: a - a = 0 is divisible by 5. Symmetric: 5 | (a - b) implies 5 | (b - a). Transitive: 5 | (a - b) and 5 | (b - c) imply 5 | (a - c) = (a - b) + (b - c). Classes: [0], [1], [2], [3], [4], the residues modulo 5.

### Q2

**Numerical · 3 marks · medium · Apply · CO1 · Unit 1: “Closure operations”**

R = {(1,2), (2,3), (3,4)} is a relation on {1, 2, 3, 4}. How many ordered pairs does the transitive closure of R contain?


**Answer:** 6

**Model answer:** Transitive closure adds (1,3), (2,4), (1,4): {(1,2), (2,3), (3,4), (1,3), (2,4), (1,4)}, 6 pairs.

### Q3

**True/False · 2 marks · medium · Analyze · CO1 · Unit 1: “Partially ordered Sets and Lattices”**

True or False: The set {1, 2, 3, 6, 12, 18} partially ordered by divisibility is a lattice.


**Answer:** False

**Model answer:** False. 12 and 18 have common multiples only outside the set (36), so they have no least upper bound in the set.

### Q4

**Numerical · 2 marks · easy · Apply · CO1 · Unit 1: “Composition of functions and Inverse functions”**

Let f(x) = 2x + 3 and g(x) = x^2 on the real numbers. Find the value of (f o g)(2), where (f o g)(x) = f(g(x)).


**Answer:** 11

**Model answer:** g(2) = 4, f(4) = 2 x 4 + 3 = 11.

### Q5

**MCQ · 1 mark · easy · Understand · CO1 · Unit 1: “Relations - properties”**

The relation R = {(1,1), (2,2), (3,3), (1,2), (2,1)} on the set {1, 2, 3} is:

- (A) Reflexive and symmetric but not transitive
- (B) An equivalence relation
- (C) A partial order
- (D) Neither reflexive nor symmetric

**Answer:** (B) An equivalence relation

**Model answer:** It is reflexive, symmetric and transitive, hence an equivalence relation (classes {1,2} and {3}).

### Q6

**Numerical · 3 marks · medium · Apply · CO2 · Unit 2: “Conditional Probability”**

Two fair dice are rolled. Given that the first die shows an even number, what is the probability that the sum is 8? Give the answer as a decimal.


**Answer:** 0.1667 (± 0.001)

**Model answer:** Outcomes with the first die even: 18. Of these, sum 8: (2,6), (4,4), (6,2), 3 outcomes. P = 3/18 = 1/6 = 0.1667.

### Q7

**Numerical · 3 marks · medium · Apply · CO2 · Unit 2: “Distributions: Binomial”**

Each item from a machine is defective with probability 0.1, independently. In a sample of 5 items, what is the probability that exactly 2 are defective?


**Answer:** 0.0729 (± 0.0001)

**Model answer:** P = C(5,2) (0.1)^2 (0.9)^3 = 10 x 0.01 x 0.729 = 0.0729.

### Q8

**Numerical · 3 marks · medium · Apply · CO2 · Unit 2: “expected values, mean, variance and standard deviation”**

A random variable X takes the values 0, 1 and 2 with probabilities 0.2, 0.5 and 0.3. Find the variance of X.


**Answer:** 0.49 (± 0.001)

**Model answer:** E[X] = 0.5 + 0.6 = 1.1; E[X^2] = 0.5 + 1.2 = 1.7; Var = 1.7 - 1.21 = 0.49.

### Q9

**Short Answer · 3 marks · medium · Understand · CO2 · Unit 2: “Poisson”**

Explain when the Poisson distribution is a good approximation to the binomial distribution, and give its parameter in terms of n and p.


**Model answer:** When n is large and p is small (rare events) with np moderate; the Poisson parameter is lambda = np, and P(X = k) = e^(-lambda) lambda^k / k!.

### Q10

**Short Answer · 3 marks · easy · Apply · CO3 · Unit 3: “Basic Connectives and Truth Tables”**

Construct the truth table of (p -> q) AND (q -> p) and show that it is logically equivalent to p <-> q.


**Model answer:** Rows (p,q): TT -> T, TF -> F, FT -> F, FF -> T for both expressions, so the columns are identical and the statements are equivalent.

### Q11

**Derivation · 4 marks · medium · Apply · CO3 · Unit 3: “Logical Equivalence”**

Using the laws of logic, show that NOT (p OR (NOT p AND q)) is logically equivalent to NOT p AND NOT q, naming the law used at each step.


**Model answer:** NOT(p OR (NOT p AND q)) = NOT p AND NOT(NOT p AND q) [De Morgan] = NOT p AND (p OR NOT q) [De Morgan, double negation] = (NOT p AND p) OR (NOT p AND NOT q) [distributive] = F OR (NOT p AND NOT q) [negation law] = NOT p AND NOT q [identity].

### Q12

**MCQ · 1 mark · easy · Understand · CO3 · Unit 3: “Rules of Inference”**

'If it rains, the match is cancelled. The match was not cancelled. Therefore it did not rain.' Which rule of inference is used?

- (A) Modus ponens
- (B) Modus tollens
- (C) Hypothetical syllogism
- (D) Disjunctive syllogism

**Answer:** (B) Modus tollens

**Model answer:** From p -> q and NOT q, conclude NOT p: modus tollens.

### Q13

**Proof · 6 marks · medium · Apply · CO3 · Unit 3: “Principle of Induction”**

Prove by mathematical induction that 3 divides n^3 - n for every integer n >= 1.


**Model answer:** Base: n = 1 gives 0, divisible by 3. Step: assume 3 | k^3 - k. Then (k+1)^3 - (k+1) = (k^3 - k) + 3k^2 + 3k, a sum of multiples of 3, so 3 divides it. Hence true for all n >= 1.

### Q14

**MCQ · 2 marks · medium · Understand · CO4 · Unit 4: “Lagrange's Theorem”**

By Lagrange's theorem, which of the following cannot be the order of a subgroup of a group of order 12?

- (A) 3
- (B) 4
- (C) 5
- (D) 6

**Answer:** (C) 5

**Model answer:** The order of a subgroup divides the order of the group; 5 does not divide 12.

### Q15

**Numerical · 4 marks · hard · Apply · CO5 · Unit 4: “Inclusion Exclusion Principles”**

How many integers from 1 to 1000 are divisible by 3, 5 or 7?


**Answer:** 543

**Model answer:** 333 + 200 + 142 - 66 - 47 - 28 + 9 = 543 (multiples of 3, 5, 7; of 15, 21, 35; of 105).

### Q16

**Numerical · 2 marks · easy · Apply · CO5 · Unit 4: “Pigeonhole principle”**

What is the minimum number of students in a class that guarantees at least 3 of them were born in the same month?


**Answer:** 25

**Model answer:** Generalised pigeonhole: 2 x 12 + 1 = 25.

### Q17

**Derivation · 6 marks · hard · Apply · CO5 · Unit 4: “Basics of recurrence relations”**

Solve the recurrence a(n) = 5a(n-1) - 6a(n-2) with a(0) = 1 and a(1) = 4, and use your solution to find a(4).


**Model answer:** Characteristic equation r^2 - 5r + 6 = 0, roots 2 and 3: a(n) = A 2^n + B 3^n. A + B = 1, 2A + 3B = 4 give B = 2, A = -1. a(n) = 2 x 3^n - 2^n; a(4) = 162 - 16 = 146.

### Q18

**Assertion-Reason · 2 marks · medium · Analyze · CO6 · Unit 5: “Euler and Hamilton paths”**

Read the assertion and reason about euler and Hamilton paths and choose the correct option.

- **Assertion (A):** The complete graph K4 has no Euler circuit.
- **Reason (R):** Every vertex of K4 has odd degree.
- (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
- (B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
- (C) A is true, but R is false
- (D) A is false, but R is true

**Answer:** (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A

**Model answer:** A connected graph has an Euler circuit iff every vertex has even degree; in K4 each vertex has degree 3, so both are true and R explains A.

### Q19

**Short Answer · 4 marks · medium · Analyze · CO6 · Unit 5: “graph isomorphism”**

G1 is the cycle on 6 vertices and G2 consists of two disjoint triangles. Both have 6 vertices, 6 edges and every vertex of degree 2. Are they isomorphic? Justify using a graph invariant.


**Model answer:** No. Isomorphism preserves connectivity (and cycle lengths); G1 is connected with a 6-cycle, G2 has two components and only 3-cycles.

### Q20

**Case Study · 10 marks · hard · Create · CO6 · Unit 5: “Tree properties, traversal techniques”**

> **Case:** A storage server holds a folder hierarchy: each folder can contain files (with sizes) and sub-folders, and there are no links between folders. The administrator needs the total size of every folder, including everything inside its sub-folders.

Model the case as a rooted tree, state the tree properties you rely on, and design a traversal that computes the total size of every folder. Justify the traversal order you choose.


**Model answer:** Folders and files are vertices, containment edges; with no cross links it is a tree (connected, acyclic, n vertices and n - 1 edges, unique path from the root). A postorder traversal computes each child's total before its parent, so a folder's size = own files + sum of sub-folder totals, in O(n) time.
